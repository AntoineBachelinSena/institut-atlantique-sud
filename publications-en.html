Working Papers Empirical research with replication data and code
IAS Working Paper 2026/01

Who measures conversion: testability, blindness and the missing denominator in Brazil’s critical-minerals doctrine

Antoine Bachelin Sena 8 September 2026 DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22567731 Licence CC BY 4.0

Brazil’s critical-minerals doctrine names domestic processing as its goal but supplies no measure by which anyone could later establish whether it occurred. This working paper proposes a falsifiable test of conversion and applies it to Comex Stat monthly data for 2016–2025, across eight tariff lines covering lithium, rare earths, niobium and magnets. The test can be run on one line only, spodumene concentrate, and that line returns a commodity reading: no Brazilian line currently validates conversion. The other seven are blind for three distinct reasons — nomenclature, missing industry, market structure — and only the first can be fixed by a customs code. A second route, using destination structure alone, discriminates more cleanly than the price route. The Minaçu rare-earth case, whose operator Serra Verde passed to USA Rare Earth on 3 September 2026, is examined in detail.

STRATEGIC POSITION NOTE · JUNE 2026

The French strategic context on critical minerals and Guyana

This note examines the unprecedented convergence, between 2022 and 2026, of the Varin report, the creation of DIAMMS, the reform of the mining code and Senate report no. 135 on the Guiana Shield. It analyses the structural bottlenecks that weaken this window of opportunity and proposes concrete recommendations to secure it for the benefit of French and European sovereignty.
